Raah: a low-cost walking guide for blind people

A phone app, worn in a chest pouch, that warns about what is ahead and says what it is, in Hindi. Warnings work with no internet. The cloud is used only when the person asks "what is this?". It costs the user almost nothing. Raah (राह) means "path".

Like you're five: imagine a friend walking just behind you who keeps quiet most of the time. When something is in your way, they say "auto ahead, close, on your left." When you ask "what's this shop?", they read the board for you. Raah is that friend, living in a phone that hangs on your chest.

Under ₹500cost to the user at first: a pouch and one earbud, on a phone they own
0.3 secondstarget time from seeing a hazard to buzzing, about 40 cm of walking
Offlineevery safety warning works with no internet

Why

The problem

India has millions of blind and low-vision people. Most use a white cane, which finds things at foot level but can't warn about a vehicle reversing toward them, a branch at head height, or say which bus has arrived. Devices that help exist, but they cost ₹2–4 lakh or are built for Western streets and English. Indian streets are harder: often no footpath, mixed traffic, parked bikes, open drains, speed breakers and stray animals.

The gap in one line: describing a picture is a solved problem. Guiding someone safely along an Indian street, in their language, at a price they can pay, is not.

Reuse first

What already exists

ToolWhat it doesWhat it lacks for this job
Be My Eyes, Seeing AI, Google LookoutFree apps that read text and describe a photoThe phone is held in the hand, and you have to ask. No warnings while walking. We recommend them alongside Raah.
RBI MANI appIdentifies Indian currency notesOnly money. Raah links to it rather than rebuilding it.
Smart glasses (Envision, OrCam, Ray-Ban Meta)Camera glasses that read or describe₹30k to ₹4 lakh, mostly English, often need internet
IIT Delhi SmartCaneCane vibrates for obstacles at body heightDoesn't say what the thing is. It proves Indian users take up low-cost aids.

What's new

The part nobody has built yet

  • Hands-free warnings that run all the time and speak only when something matters.
  • Trained on Indian streets: autos, e-rickshaws, cows, dogs, open drains, speed breakers, handcarts, not just "car" and "person".
  • Hindi first, offline first. Other languages later.
  • Free and open source. A clip-on camera comes later, given free where possible through the government's ADIP scheme and NGOs.

How it works

Two paths

The core

Knowing when to stay quiet

The hardest problem is talking too much, not detecting things. A street has dozens of objects. If the app names them all, the person stops listening, and their ears are what they rely on to hear traffic. So every detection gets one of three levels.

LevelMeaningWhat the person hears
StopCould hurt within about 2 seconds: a vehicle within 3 m and coming closer, an open drain or step down ahead, a bar at head heightA strong double buzz and one or two words, "Ruko! Naali". It interrupts anything else.
In pathIn the walking line but not urgent: a parked bike, a pole, a cowA short phrase with side and distance, "Pole, seedha, 4 kadam", said once.
InfoUseful but not in the way: a door, a bench, a bus stop signOnly in explore mode, or when asked.

Non-negotiable

Safety rules

  1. Never say "the path is clear." Silence doesn't mean safe, and users learn that on day one.
  2. The cane stays. Raah adds to the white cane and never replaces it.
  3. Ears stay open: one earbud or bone-conduction headphones only.
  4. Fail loudly. If the camera is blocked, the battery is low or the phone overheats, it says so.
  5. A missed hazard counts far more than a false alarm. Better to warn about a shadow than miss a drain.

The plan

Five phases, each with a pass or fail test

PhaseWhenWhatPasses if
0 · ListenNov 2026Walk real routes with 8–10 blind people through blind-welfare groups. Ask what goes wrong and what would help.A ranked list of their top 3 needs. If walking hazards aren't in it, the plan changes.
1 · Phone appDec 2026 – Jan 2027Camera, ready-made detector, rule table, Hindi voice, buzz, and "ye kya hai?"Warning under 0.4 s, 2 hours of battery, 3 blind users use it alone after 15 minutes.
2 · Indian streetsFeb – Mar 2027Retrain the detector on Indian street images and our own walk videos; add step-down detectionCatches 95% of stop-level hazards in test videos, at most 1 false alarm a minute.
3 · PilotApr – Jun 202720 people, 4 weeks of daily use, a sighted helper nearby at first12 of 20 still use it in week 4, and no safety incident caused by the app.
4 · Clip-on cameraJul – Dec 2027A spectacle camera that follows the head; more languages; NGOs hand it outCamera under ₹3k to make, and at least one NGO giving it out.

Accuracy is a number, not a feeling: 200 short walk videos filmed from chest height on Indian streets, by day, night and in rain, with every hazard marked by hand. Every new version is scored on them before any user sees it.

Want to help? Raah needs Android developers, people to film and label street videos, and above all blind and low-vision people and the groups that work with them, to tell us what matters. Write to basant@cleandataengineering.ai and say what you would like to pick up.
