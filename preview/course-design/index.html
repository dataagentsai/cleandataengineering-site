4 · The 10-minute picture

The harness answers seven questions

Three jobs, seven questions. Every part of the harness answers exactly one of them. The first job matches what every vendor guide calls an agent (model, instructions, tools, loop); the other two are what production adds.

Do the work what the guides call "the agent"

1 · How does it decide?

  • Routing: does this even need the model?
  • Model calls: which model, through which door
  • The loop: plan, act, check, stop
  • Patterns: workflow or agent, one agent or many

2 · What does it know?

  • Instructions (prompts, versioned)
  • Context: what goes into the window
  • Knowledge: finding the right documents
  • Memory: what it remembers next time

3 · What can it do?

  • Tools: actions, MCP, safe writes, sandboxed code
Earn trust what production adds, part 1

4 · Is it safe?

  • Guardrails, including prompt injection
  • Identity and permissions
  • Privacy and personal data
  • Human approval and handoff
  • Audit trail

5 · Does it keep working?

  • Failure handling: retries, fallbacks, no double actions
  • Durable state: it crashed at step 7, now what?
  • Speed and scale: latency, load, limits, caching
  • Availability and recovery
Get better what production adds, part 2

6 · Can I see what it did, and what it cost?

  • Tracing and replay
  • Cost

7 · Is it good, and can I change it safely?

  • Evaluation: before release, in production, from feedback
  • Release and versioning: prompt, model and config together

Around the harness, drawn outside the box: the front door (channels), your business systems and data, ordinary software engineering, and the LLM Platform.

6 · Around the harness

What sits outside the box

Your system

  • Front door: chat, email, phone, apps, streaming the answer
  • Business systems and data: what the tools reach
  • It's still software: APIs and typed contracts, config and secrets, CI/CD, infrastructure as code, the test pyramid

LLM Platform

  • Models: which brain
  • Providers: who trains and serves it
  • Access and gateway: one door, routing, caching, quotas
  • Compute: where it physically runs
  • The AI Universe page explains the layers in detail

Non-functional requirements and cross-cutting concerns aren't a fourth box. They're the "Earn trust" and "Get better" jobs. Architects who want the formal names (ISO 25010: security, reliability, performance, and so on) get a mapping table at the 5-hour level. Beginners never need it.

8 · Decide together

Open questions

  1. Metaphor: the car, the body, or none?The car matches your AI Universe line ("the engine, not the car"). Google uses a body (brain, hands, nervous system). A metaphor makes doodles easy but can be stretched too far.
  2. Seven questions: right number?Could go to six by merging 6 and 7 ("Can I see it and improve it?").
  3. Do the work / Earn trust / Get better: are those the right three job names?Alternatives: Build / Protect / Improve, or Think & act / Stay safe & steady / Learn.
  4. Model calls: inside the harness, or drawn as the bridge between the boxes?Inside keeps the harness complete; a bridge makes the two-box picture clearer.
  5. Does "Do I even need an agent?" come early (big picture) or inside question 1 (patterns)?Early is the deterministic-first message; inside is tidier.
  6. Are the formal NFR names shown to learners at all?Proposed: only at the 5-hour level, as a mapping table for architects.
