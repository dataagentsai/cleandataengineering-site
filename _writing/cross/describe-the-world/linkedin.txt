Your agent has 200 test scripts. Then the rules change, or a new kind of failure shows up, and someone has to remember which scripts to edit. There's a better unit of work: describe the agent's world once, and generate the scenarios from it. Here is what that description holds.

Writing hundreds of test scripts for an agent doesn't scale, because every new rule or fault means editing scripts by hand. Instead, write down the agent's world once: its users, systems and data, rules, faults, approvers and time. Scenarios are then generated from that description, including combinations nobody would have thought to script. This post shows what a good world description holds, with a small example, and says honestly what AgentTwin's format does today.
