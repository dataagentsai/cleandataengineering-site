Session 3 · What goes wrong

The hardest challenges in data projects

Data projects rarely fail because the code was hard. They fail on knowledge nobody wrote down, people who are not on your team, and time spent waiting. Ten challenges, each with its early sign and what helps.

Teach first

Three groups

Knowledge, people and time. Only a small part of the work is the code.

In one line: the hard parts are knowing, agreeing and waiting. Plan for them in the backlog, as work.

The ten

Challenge by challenge

1Nobody knows what the source means Knowledge

Why it is hard
The knowledge is in a few heads, or in nobody's.
Early sign
Fields nobody can explain; 'ask Ravi, but he left'.
What helps
Meaning test in week 1 to 2; a named owner per source; write answers down. Read more →

2Old reports hide the rules Knowledge

Why it is hard
The current report is a spreadsheet with years of manual fixes nobody documented.
Early sign
'The number is right, don't touch it', but nobody can say how it is made.
What helps
Reverse-engineer the report with its owner before designing anything. Read more →

3The data is worse than expected Knowledge

Why it is hard
Real data has duplicates, gaps and odd codes that samples don't show.
Early sign
Profiling in week 2 shows 15 percent of rows break a rule.
What helps
Profile real data in discovery, and agree what to do with bad rows. Read more →

4Other teams own the sources People

Why it is hard
They have their own priorities and no reason to help you quickly.
Early sign
Requests wait; answers come in weeks.
What helps
Sponsor-backed requests, small and early, with dates. Treat source owners as stakeholders. Read more →

5Departments define things differently People

Why it is hard
Revenue, customer and active mean different things to sales and finance.
Early sign
Two reports, two numbers, one argument.
What helps
One owner per metric; show both numbers on real data; the owner decides; write it down. Read more →

6Requirements change when people see data People

Why it is hard
Nobody knows what they want until they see their own numbers.
Early sign
'This isn't what I meant' at acceptance.
What helps
Show real numbers in week two and every two weeks after. Slice thin. Read more →

7Access and approvals take weeks Time

Why it is hard
Security, privacy and network changes follow their own calendar.
Early sign
The team has nothing to work on in week one.
What helps
Send requests on day one, with dates; track waiting separately from effort. Read more →

8The unknown is hard to estimate Time

Why it is hard
Estimates count code, and the real work is learning and waiting.
Early sign
The first number is two to three times too low.
What helps
Ranges, effort and calendar apart, estimate discovery, re-estimate after the first slice. Read more →

9Proving the numbers is slow Time

Why it is hard
Reconciling to finance finds differences that need a person to explain.
Early sign
Last week of the project: totals differ by 3 percent.
What helps
Plan reconciliation as a stage from slice one, not a final week. Read more →

10Nobody owns it after go-live People

Why it is hard
The team leaves; operations and the business are not ready.
Early sign
A job fails on Sunday and no one is alerted.
What helps
Operations at demos from MVP 1; a rehearsed failure drill; named owners. Read more →

FAQ

Questions people ask

What is the hardest part of a data project?

Usually not the code. It is learning what the source data means, and getting people in other teams to agree on definitions and spare time. Both are about people and knowledge, so they have to be planned for.

How can I tell early that a project is in trouble?

Three early signs: nobody can name the person who understands a source, no one has seen real data yet, and access has not been requested. Any one is worth raising the same day.
