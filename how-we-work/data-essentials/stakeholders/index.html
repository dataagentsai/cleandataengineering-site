Topic 3 · People

Stakeholders in a data project

A data project depends on more people outside the team than most projects. This page shows who they are, what each needs from you, and how to keep them with you without long meetings.

The map

Who matters, and how much

The grid below is the standard way to sort people by how much they can change your project and how much they care about it. Data projects have one twist.

The twist: source system owners can change your plan the most, and care about your project the least.

In one line: spend your effort where influence is high and interest is low. That is the source owner, security and finance, not the sponsor who is already watching.

One by one

What each needs, and what you need from each

WhoTheir roleYou needThey need from youThe usual trap
SponsorPays, and decides go, change or stop at each gate.Honest risk and progress; a clear decision to make.Decisions on scope, money and priority; air cover when a team is slow.Told only good news until it is too late.
Business ownersOwn the question, the metric definitions and the acceptance.Their time, in the week it is needed. Sign-off on definitions.Early mock-ups; demos with real numbers; plain words.Appear at acceptance and change the question.
Source system ownersRun the systems the data comes from. Not on your team, with their own priorities.Explanations, test data, access, a change calendar, a notice promise.A clear, small, early request. Their change windows respected. Thanks.First asked in month three, after the plan is signed.
Security, privacy, complianceProtect people and the company; can stop a release.Approvals, rules for personal data, access reviews.Sensitive-data list, data flow picture, early review.Brought in at the end, when they can only say no.
FinanceOwns the numbers the business trusts; often the budget.Figures to reconcile against; sign-off that totals match.A reconciliation plan and cost forecast.Discovers the totals do not match at go-live.
Build teamDesigns, builds, tests and deploys.Clear priorities, answers within a day, a protected backlog.Shielding from scope creep; the chasing done for them.Becomes the one chasing sources and sponsors.

The weekly note

Status that tells the truth

One page, five headings, every week, to everyone above. Keep the headings the same, so readers learn where to look.

  1. Proved this week. What now works end to end, with a number someone checked. Not 'percent complete'.
  2. Next. What we will prove next, in one or two lines.
  3. Risks, with a date. What could hurt us, who owns it, and by when we need an answer.
  4. Waiting on. Names of people we are waiting on, and since when.
  5. Decisions needed. What you need from the reader, by when, and what happens if it is late.

'Percent complete' is not on the list. A data project that is 80 percent built can still be 0 percent usable. Report what was proved.

FAQ

Questions people ask

Who is the most forgotten stakeholder?

The source system owner. They have the most influence on your plan, the least interest in your project, and they are first asked too late.

How often should I update the sponsor?

Weekly in writing, in five lines, plus a short call when a decision is needed. Fewer surprises matter more than longer reports.
