Reviews and sign-offs, internal and external

What to review, who reviews it, how to run a review, what goes wrong, and who signs what. Includes reviews by the platform vendor and the cloud provider for architecture and design.

Teach first

A review is a planned moment to be told you are wrong, cheaply

A review is when someone who did not build the thing reads it against agreed criteria, with the power to say stop. A sign-off is the decision that follows. The cheapest time to find a flaw is before it is built. A review exists to move that moment earlier.

In one line: reviews find problems, sign-offs take responsibility. Both need evidence, a named person and a date.

Who reviews

Five kinds, from self-check to external expert

Each step up adds independence and weight, and costs more. Use the lowest step that matches the risk.

KindWhoWhenPurposeIndependence
Self-checkThe authorBefore sharing anythingCatch your own slips with a short checklist.None: the author checks their own work.
Peer reviewA colleague on the teamEvery change and documentA second pair of eyes on detail: code, queries, definitions.Low: same team, shared assumptions.
Internal formal reviewTech Anchor, architect, BA lead, test lead, from outside the working groupAt each gate: discovery, design, test, readinessCheck the whole against criteria, from someone who did not build it.Medium: same company, different seat.
Client review and sign-offThe client's owners: business, IT, security, finance, operationsAt each gateThe people who will live with it confirm it fits.High for fit; they may lack technical depth.
External expert reviewPlatform vendor, cloud provider, independent assessor, auditor, regulatorDesign (stage 4), readiness (stage 8), and when risk is highSpecialist knowledge and independence.High; but vendors have a commercial interest.

How to review

Five steps, and a way to rate findings

Prepare, read alone, meet, decide, follow up.

  1. Prepare. Send the pack three working days ahead, with the criteria. One short summary page on top.
  2. Read alone. Each reviewer reads and logs findings before the meeting.
  3. Meet. 30 to 60 minutes on findings only. No presentations. Decide, do not debate.
  4. Decide. Approve, approve with conditions, or reject. Say which.
  5. Follow up. Each finding has an owner and a date. Re-review the blockers.

Rate every finding

SeverityMeaning
BlockerStops the gate. Must be fixed before proceeding.
MajorFix before go-live, or accept in writing with a date.
MinorFix when convenient. Track it.
NoteAn observation. No action needed.

A review checklist makes this repeatable. The project review checks are written as checks you can reuse as criteria. Checks as code, and the Ops gate explains how.

External

Platform vendor and cloud review of the architecture and design

Platform vendors and cloud providers often offer a review of how you plan to use their product. It is worth having, if you use it well.

The vendor advises. We decide and stay accountable.

What to ask them to review

Area to reviewWhat it coversThe question
Account, network and identity layoutHow accounts, workspaces, networks and identities are organised.Does the layout match our environments and our security rules?
Data governance and accessCatalogue, permissions, masking, audit.Can we enforce who sees what, and prove it?
Ingestion and pipeline approachHow data lands and moves.Does the pattern fit our sources, volumes and freshness?
Data layout and file designTable design, file sizes, partitioning.Will it stay fast and cheap as it grows?
Compute choice and sizingWhich compute, how big, when it starts and stops.Is it right-sized, and what does it cost per run?
Cost controlsTags, budgets, alerts, chargeback.Can we see and cap the bill by team and workload?
Release and environmentsDeployment pipeline, environment parity, configuration.Can we release safely, with rollback?
Reliability, backup and recoveryFailure handling, backup, recovery targets.What happens in a failure, and how fast do we recover?
Monitoring and operabilityLogs, metrics, alerts, runbooks.Can operations see and fix problems without the builders?
Limits, quotas and known constraintsService limits and fair-use rules.Will we hit a limit at our volume?
Support plan and escalationSupport tiers, response times, named contacts.Who do we call at 3 a.m., and what do they promise?
Roadmap and deprecationsFeatures coming, features going.Are we building on something being retired?

How to prepare

  1. Share a design pack: context, requirements, volumes, non-functional needs, the design and the alternatives you rejected.
  2. Agree scope and confidentiality in writing first. Share design and metadata, not real data.
  3. Ask for findings in writing, rated by severity, with the reasoning.
  4. Ask what the review does not cover. Vendors review how their product is used, not your business rules.
  5. Invite the people who will run it, not only the designers.
  6. Record each finding as accepted, rejected or deferred, with a reason, in the decision log.

Cautions

CautionWhy
The vendor has a commercial interestAdvice may favour more of their product. Ask for the cheapest design that meets the need, and a second opinion on big spend.
A vendor review is not your sign-offYou stay accountable. A vendor's 'looks fine' does not transfer responsibility.
A review of the design is not a review of the dataVendors rarely see your real data problems. Keep profiling and reconciliation with us.
Review services differ by agreementCheck what your contract includes, how long it takes to book, and who from the vendor attends.
Best-practice frameworks helpCloud providers publish best-practice frameworks for security, reliability, cost and operations, and platform vendors publish similar guidance. Use them as a checklist, and still think.

Seven rules for sign-offs

  • The signer has read it. Ask them to say what they are signing.
  • One person is accountable. Others are consulted.
  • 'Conditional' means named conditions, with owners and dates.
  • Silence is not a signature.
  • The sign-off lists the evidence it rests on.
  • A sign-off can be withdrawn if the evidence changes.
  • Record it in one register: what, who, when, conditions.

FAQ

Questions people ask

Who should review the design?

Someone who did not build it: a peer architect or Tech Anchor from another project, plus the client's architect and security. For a platform design, ask the platform vendor and the cloud provider too.

Is a platform vendor review worth it?

Usually yes for a new platform design, because they know limits and best practice that you may not. Treat it as advice with a commercial interest, record each finding as a decision, and keep your own accountability.

How many sign-offs are too many?

Use the seven above for a typical project, and skip any that the risk does not justify. Fewer, with real evidence, beat many with signatures only.
