Topic 12 · Across all layers

Across everything

Four jobs that sit across every layer: quality, security, cost, and running the thing every day.

In plain words

What happens here

Think of a city's water. The lab that tests the water, the guards at the plant, the budget, and the night crew. None of them appear on the map of pipes, and the supply fails without them.

Quality is checking the data every day, not once. Security is who may see what, and protecting personal data. Cost is the cloud bill, which grows quietly. Running is the schedule, the alerts, and the person who answers at 2 a.m. These are planned from the first week, not added before go-live.

Four jobs under every layer. Each needs an owner and a place in the backlog.

Technical names you may hear for this layer: quality, security, cost, operations.

What matters most

Three things

1An owner for each of the four

Quality, security, cost and running each have a named person. A job with no owner does not get done.

2Planned in the backlog

Each has stories, estimates and acceptance criteria, like any feature. If it is not in the backlog, it is not in the plan.

3Rehearsed before go-live

A failure drill: something breaks on purpose, and operations fixes it with the runbook, without the builders.

Where it goes wrong

How this layer fails

What goes wrongWhat it looks likeEarly warning signWhat it costs
Personal data found lateSensitive fields are in a test environment, or a report shows them to everyone.Nobody asked 'is any of this personal or regulated?' in discovery.A stop, a clean-up and sometimes a report to a regulator.
A surprise cloud billThe monthly cost is several times the estimate.Nobody owns the cost, and nothing alerts on a jump.Funding questions, then pressure to cut things that matter.
Nobody is on callA job fails on Sunday and the first to notice is an executive with a blank dashboard.The runbook is written by the builders and never rehearsed.The project's reputation, after go-live.

Causes from the failure list

  • 7. Access and approvals arrive late: Weeks lost waiting for a key.
  • 24. Nobody owns it after go-live: The team left. The job failed on Sunday.
  • 25. No monitoring, runbook or failure drill: It fails silently.
  • 26. Cost surprise: The bill is three times the plan.
  • 28. Sensitive data found late: A stop, a clean-up and a call to a regulator.

Dependencies

What and who you depend on

  • Security, privacy and compliance teams.
  • The cloud cost owner (often finance or a platform team).
  • The operations or support team that will run it.
  • Monitoring and alerting set up by the build team.

In the room

Four questions to ask

  1. Is any of this data personal, confidential or regulated?
  2. What is the monthly budget, and who watches it?
  3. Who is alerted when a job fails, and what do they do?
  4. How will we show data quality every day, to the business?
Done means: Four named owners. Quality checks run daily and are visible. A cost budget with an alert. A rehearsed failure drill.
