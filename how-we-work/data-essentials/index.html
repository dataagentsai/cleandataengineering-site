Data project essentials, for non-technical roles

How a data project is run, from a client's first request to the last dashboard: who does what, what goes wrong, what to ask in each layer, and a reusable backlog. No code. Fifteen topics, one idea each, with a picture for every idea.

The idea in one line: data projects rarely fail on code. They fail on what nobody asked, who nobody named, and what nobody proved.

What matters most

Ten sentences to remember

  1. Data projects are discovery first and delivery second.
  2. The source is the biggest unknown, and someone else owns it.
  3. Slice thin: one source to one trusted number, then widen.
  4. Every number has a name, a formula and an owner.
  5. Done means the totals match a figure the business already trusts.
  6. Raise every access and approval on day one, with a date.
  7. Estimate in ranges, and keep effort apart from calendar time.
  8. Say bad news early. It is the cheapest it will ever be.
  9. Plan the running before go-live: who is alerted, who fixes it.
  10. Switch off the old report, or you have two truths.

What makes a data project fail

Six ways of not knowing

  • A. We don't know the data: Source meaning unknown, Changes and deletes not understood, Data quality worse than expected, The old report's logic is hidden, Volume and timing…
  • B. We don't have the people: Source owners not engaged, Access and approvals arrive late, No owner for each metric, One person holds the knowledge, Vendors and third parties are o…
  • C. We don't agree: Departments define metrics differently, Requirements change when people see real data, Scope grows silently, Sponsor, PM and team expect different thi…
  • D. We don't prove it: No reconciliation to a trusted figure, Acceptance late, or on fake data, Wrong grain for the question, No daily data quality checks…
  • E. We don't plan for reality: Estimates count code, not learning and waiting, Discovery skipped, Built layer by layer, then a big-bang go-live, Status reported as percent complete,…
  • F. We don't run and adopt it: Nobody owns it after go-live, No monitoring, runbook or failure drill, Cost surprise, Old report not retired, so adoption is low, Sensitive data found…

The full list, with early signs and what prevents each, is on Why data projects fail.

FAQ

Questions people ask

Do I need to know how to code?

No. Nothing here needs code. Where a page links to technical material, it is marked optional.

How long is the whole programme?

Fifteen topics of about an hour. Pages can also be read alone once you have seen the big picture.

What is the Tech Anchor?

The person who holds the technical shape of the project together, end to end. Sometimes the title is tech lead or delivery architect. The seats page explains the role.

Which pages are new and which already existed?

The roadmap marks each link: 'new' was written for this programme, 'existing' is reused as it is, 'technical, optional' is for your engineers.
