Part 1 · The source system

#QuestionWhy we askYour answer
A · Ownership and changewho answers questions and who tells us about changes
A1Who owns this system, and who knows its data best?Someone has to answer 'is this value right?' and approve fixes on the source side.
A2When does the source change (releases, upgrades, patches), and who tells us beforehand?A column renamed on their side is a broken pipeline on ours.
A3Is this system being replaced, migrated or merged in the next year?A deep integration with a system that retires in six months is wasted work.
A4What is the source's own support window and service level?Our freshness promise can never beat theirs.
B · Access routehow we are allowed to reach the data
B1How can we reach the data: direct database read, a read replica, an API, files, an event stream, or a share?The route decides the tools, the security work and the cost.
B2Can we read from a replica, standby or backup instead of the live system?Our extract must never slow down their customers.
B3What are the limits: API rate limits, rows per call, extract windows, connection counts?Limits decide how long a backfill takes and how we page and retry.
B4What network path and credentials do we need: private link or VPN, firewall rules, a service account, password rotation?These requests are slow; they decide the start date, not the code.
C · Push supportwhat the source can send us without being asked
C1Can the source push changes: log-based CDC, a vendor extractor, business events, webhooks, or files it drops?Push captures every change, including deletes, as it happens, without scanning tables.
C2For CDC: is the transaction log switched on at the level we need, and how long is it kept?If the log is cleared before we read it, those changes are gone for good.
C3For events and webhooks: is delivery at least once, is order kept, can we replay, is there an event id and a sequence number?These answers decide how we remove duplicates and put changes in order.
C4Does the source say when a batch is complete: a done file, a row count, an event?Without it we cannot tell a half-written file from a whole one.
D · Pull supportwhat we can fetch, and whether we can trust it
D1Is there a column that reliably says when each row last changed?Incremental loads depend on it completely.
D2Is that column set by the database on every change, including bulk fixes and scripts, or by the app?App-set times skip changes made outside the app.
D3Can the API filter by changed-since time, and does it page in a stable order?Paging by position over changing data skips or repeats rows.
D4Can we take a full snapshot, how long does it take, and when may we?A full read is the safety net for checking incremental loads and finding deletes.
E · How the data changesthe answers that decide full versus incremental and how silver merges (answer per dataset)
E1Which of these happen: inserts only, updates, hard deletes, soft deletes?This decides the load method and how the next layer merges rows.
E2Can old records change: late arrivals, backdated entries, corrections to a closed period? How far back?This sets how far back each load looks, and when a period counts as final.
E3Does the source ever resend or restate a whole period, or the whole table?A resend must replace, not add to, what we already hold.
E4Can a business key change, be reused, or be merged (two customer records joined into one)?History must follow the real thing, not the number.
E5Are any changes made outside the app, such as data fixes, migrations or mass updates?These are the changes most likely to bypass change tracking.
F · Keys and dateswhat makes a row unique and which date means what (answer per dataset)
F1What is the business key of each dataset: one column or several? Is it really unique?Keys drive de-duplication, merging and every join after this.
F2Which date means what: transaction, effective, posting, created, modified?Reports must use the right date for the right question.
F3What time zone and precision are times stored in, and how is daylight saving handled?Rows near midnight and on clock changes go to the wrong day, or twice.
F4How do keys link across datasets and across systems (customer id in the CRM versus in billing)?Gold tables join across sources; the joins need a known mapping.
G · Schema and meaningwhat the columns and codes mean
G1Is there a data dictionary? What do the status codes and flags mean?A code means nothing to us without its definition.
G2How often does the schema change, with how much notice, and are changes only additions?Added columns are easy; renamed, retyped or removed columns break things.
G3Are there splits by company, country, tenant, currency or unit?Numbers in different currencies or units cannot be added.
G4Are there free-text fields, JSON blobs or customer-specific custom fields?They need their own parsing, and often hold personal data.
H · Volume and timinghow much, how fast and when
H1How many rows exist today, how many arrive per day, how fast is it growing, and when are the peaks?Sizing, cost and partitioning come from these numbers.
H2How much history do we need, and does the source still hold it?This decides the backfill plan.
H3When is the data ready at the source each day, and how fresh do users need it?Freshness is a chain; every link must fit.
H4Can the source tell us when data is ready, so we start on that signal instead of a clock?Starting on a signal removes most late or partial loads.
I · Access to profile real datawhy we ask to scan your data
I1Can we have a copy of production data in a sandbox: masked if it holds personal data, as is if it doesn't?Only real data shows real keys, gaps, odd values and edge cases.
I2If data cannot leave your environment, can we run profiling inside it and take only the results out?Profiles (counts, ranges, match rates) usually hold no personal data.
I3Who on your side will review the profile and confirm what is expected?Odd values are either bugs or business rules; only the owner knows which.
J · Sensitivity and ruleswhat the law and your policies require
J1Which columns hold personal, health, payment or other sensitive data?This decides masking, who may see what and where data may be stored.
J2What are the retention, deletion-on-request, residency and consent rules?A deletion request must reach every copy we hold.
J3Are there licence or contract limits on using third-party data?Some data may be used only for some purposes.
K · Trust and reconciliationhow we will prove nothing was lost
K1What quality problems do you already know about?Known problems are cheap to plan for and expensive to discover live.
K2Can the source give control totals per load, such as row counts and the sum of amounts per day?This is the only way to prove nothing was lost or doubled.
K3Which report in the source system do users trust today?Users will compare our numbers with it on day one.
