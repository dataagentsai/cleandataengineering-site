How it works

How the estimate is made

Each hop turns its data volumes into work, measured in classic DBU-hours: reading new data, merging changes (plus rewriting the files those changes land in), refreshing gold, pre-computing aggregates. The compute you choose turns work into dollars. Serverless multiplies by one DBU price that includes the machines. Classic multiplies by a lower DBU price and adds the EC2 machines, start-up time and network. SQL warehouses are priced by the hours they are on, which depends on users, schedules and auto-stop, not on the number of queries.

The work rates are calibrated defaults, not measurements of your system. Treat the result as a planning estimate (the range shown is roughly minus a third to plus a half) and confirm it against system.billing.usage once you run. The Cost & FinOps page explains the drivers and hidden costs in words.
