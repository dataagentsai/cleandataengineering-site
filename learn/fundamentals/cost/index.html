Cost fundamentals: what you pay for in a data platform

A data bill is not a mystery either. You pay for four things: compute time, stored bytes, bytes that move, and the people and services around them. Most waste is one of a short list of patterns. This page organises them as a mind map, with the matching CostSherlock rules, so you can find the leak by category.

Start here

The data physics

Five plain facts that explain almost everything below. Read these and the rest follows.

You pay for time, not work.Compute is billed by the second or hour that it is on. An idle cluster costs the same as a busy one.
Performance and cost are the same fight.A faster job usually costs less because it holds the machine for less time. The exception is paying for speed you did not need.
Storage is cheap, scanning it is not.Keeping a byte costs little. Reading it again and again, or moving it between regions, is where money goes.
Frequency multiplies everything.A job that costs 2 dollars is 730 dollars a year if it runs daily and 17,500 if it runs hourly. Ask how often before asking how fast.
What nobody owns, nobody stops.Orphaned clusters, unused dashboards and forgotten schedules run until someone is made responsible.

Compute that is on

Idle is the most common waste

A cluster that waits for work still bills. Interactive clusters left on overnight, warehouses that auto-stop after an hour, jobs that keep a cluster warm between runs: all pay for nothing.

Idle compute: the same bill for busy and idle hours. Illustrative example.

PatternWhy it costsFixVerdict
No auto-terminationCluster runs until someone remembersSet a short idle timeoutAlways fix
Long auto-stop on warehousesPays for the whole idle window each timeShorten to minutesPursue
Interactive cluster running scheduled jobsInteractive rates for non-interactive work, and it stays onMove to job computePursue
Always-warm pools nobody usesPay to keep machines readyReduce or removePursue
Continuous pipeline on batch dataStreaming cost for data that arrives hourlyTriggered, scheduled runsPursue
Orphaned clusters of departed usersNobody owns themPolicy: owner required, auto-cleanAlways fix

Refresh that is too frequent

Freshness is a product decision

Every refresh is a cost. The question is not 'can we refresh every minute?' but 'who reads it that often?'

Frequency multiplies everything. Same job, same $2: monthly is $24 a year, hourly is $17,520.

FAQ

Questions people ask

Where do most data platform bills leak?

Idle compute, over-sized compute, and the same expensive query or refresh repeated for no reader. Storage and network are usually smaller lines, but neglected tables slow everything and quietly grow.

Is a faster job always cheaper?

Usually, because machines are held for less time. Paying for a much larger cluster to finish sooner can cost more if the job does not scale with it. Measure cost per run, not just run time.

How often should a pipeline run?

As often as someone acts on the result, and no more. Ask who reads it and what they do differently with a fresher answer.

Does serverless always save money?

No. It removes start-up waits and idle waste for spiky work, and can cost more for steady heavy work. Compare on your own pattern.

What is the first thing to do about cost?

Tag everything with an owner, then look at the top ten items by spend. You cannot fix what nobody owns.

Are the sample figures real?

They are illustrative arithmetic to show how frequency multiplies a cost, not quotes. Prices differ by vendor and contract.
