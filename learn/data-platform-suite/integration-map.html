The integration map: every way into and out of Databricks, and where the data can sit

This picture puts Databricks in the middle. On the left are the ways data can come in, grouped by family, with four products placed on them. In the middle is where it can sit. On the right are the ways data goes out. Most diagrams of this kind show only one product's side. This one shows the choices together.

SAP, Salesforce, Oracle and Redshift are examples. The same rows apply to any product in those families. A circle is only filled where the route was checked against the vendor's current documentation: SAP and Salesforce zero-copy sharing, Salesforce's managed connector, Salesforce's query federation, and Redshift writing Iceberg tables. Everything else is from general knowledge and should be checked before it is promised.

Gold is not on this list because it is always materialized in our own tables. It is the small, optimized meeting place where virtual and physical inputs are joined. Publishing it safely is the subject of Write-audit-publish.

A tick means the combination makes sense, not that it is built. For example, federation can only be query in place (A), or an extract that becomes a full copy (E). A shared table can sit in any of the six placements from B to G.

Not built yet, or known gaps
  • The shared catalog, federated catalog and foreign-catalog placements are not built. Only the input gate for a shared table exists.
  • The ways out are mostly not built. Metric views ran on Azure and Databricks Free Edition. Sharing out, open-table interop, exports, reverse ETL and published events are designs.
  • Managed connectors, federation and CDC capture are not wired to the platform.
  • I have not seen a published diagram that combines ways in, placements and products like this one. Vendors draw their own side, so treat it as our own synthesis, not a standard.
