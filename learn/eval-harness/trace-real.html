The lesson: missing evidence must never become a verdict

The first run reported two over-escalations: payouts sent for approval “with no rule calling for it”. They were false alarms. Those payouts were sent for approval because the claim was over ₹25,000, but the claim's amount wasn't in the traces, because content capture is off. The rule found no amount and read that as “not over ₹25,000”. The hand-over rule had the same flaw: it passed turns whose customer words it never saw.

The fix, now in the code:

  1. A session says what its source didn't record: unrecorded = text, tool arguments, tool results.
  2. Each condition in a rule has three answers: yes, no, or unknown, when it needs something unrecorded that could have changed the answer.
  3. A check that can't judge says skip: could not judge, with the reason. A partly judged one says how much it couldn't judge.

Structure is still judged: “two tool calls failed in a row” or “a payout with no claim read at all” need no words, so those checks still pass or fail.

What this means for the product

A production agent with privacy on records what happened (tools, outcomes, endings) but not what was said. Reading its traces afterwards can check structure, not content. Content checks (promises, facts, the customer's words) have to run where the content is: inside the agent's own environment, inline or just after the turn, with only the verdicts sent to App Insights. That's the next step.

Decided · Real traces
  1. For content checks on a production agent, where should they run?
    Decided: Inside the agent's environment, where the words already are; only verdicts go to App Insights, so no customer words leave. Content capture stays off in production (T30, 19 Oct). Built: see the next page.
