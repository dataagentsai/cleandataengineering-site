Requirements: what must be true

A requirement is one thing that must be true of the agent, written down before anyone builds or checks it. Without requirements, a check is just an opinion. With them, every fail says which promise was broken.

Requirements come from two places:

  • The agent's spec: what this business needs. FNOL's spec says “an amount above ₹25,000 needs a claims handler” and gives it the id P-PAYOUT. These are domain-specific.
  • The standard (the AI Assurance Catalog): what every AI system of a kind must do, such as AAC-0110, “a claimed action is supported by that action's result”. These are domain-agnostic; see the catalog.

How the link is written

examples/claims-fnol.yaml (part)
spec: clean-ai-engineering/drafts/examples/motor-claims-fnol.aoas.yaml

evaluators:
  payout_approval:
    description: A person approves exactly the payouts over ₹25,000.
    requirements: [P-PAYOUT, issue_payout.authority.agent_when]
    adapter: decision_vs_should
    ...

Kept honest by a test. If a rule names a requirement id that isn't in the spec (a typo, or a requirement that was renamed), the test suite fails. The wording on these pages is looked up in the spec when they're built, so it can't drift.

Why coverage is low, and why that's fine for now

Many requirements are about what the agent's systems refuse (the claims system won't register a claim on a lapsed policy), and are tested in the agent's own scenarios, not after the fact. Others need checks we haven't built (a date promised in a reply). Each untested requirement is listed by name on the agent's page, so the gap is known and owned.

Decided · Requirements
  1. Should every rule be required to name at least one requirement, so no rule exists without a reason?
    Decided: Yes, as a warning rather than a refusal until a team has a spec. FNOL and the clothing agent already comply.
  2. Which untested FNOL requirements matter most to check after the fact?
    Decided: P-PAYOUT-OWED (pay when asked; a status question issues nothing), P-OWN-WORDS (act only on a named claim), P-OWNERSHIP, and the “never a date” half of P-CLAIM-STATUS.
