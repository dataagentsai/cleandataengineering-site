Streaming simulator: a faster platform lane

The platform lane takes the same readings to Power BI through bronze, silver and gold. Today the screen can show data up to about 90 minutes old. Four designs run here on one clock, so you can see where the waiting goes and what removing it costs.

Why today's lane is slow: where the time goes

Follow one reading from the press to the Power BI screen in design A. On average it takes about 60 minutes (best about 31, worst about 90). Only about 7 of those minutes are work. The rest is waiting for clocks.

So three quarters of the time comes from building gold once an hour, and most of the rest from Power BI's refresh clock. The 15-minute run cadence, the obvious suspect, is hidden inside the hourly wait; it only starts to matter after gold is chained. In design B the same reading takes about 14 minutes on average: about 7.5 waiting for the next run, 3 for the run, 2 for gold and 1.5 for the refresh. Now the cadence is the biggest share, which is what design C shortens.

Continuous Flink or continuous DLT: what each costs

Both run all day. Per CPU-hour they cost about the same; the difference is how small each can go. Flink scales down to one small processing unit. A DLT (Lakeflow Declarative Pipelines) pipeline is a Spark cluster with a driver and at least one worker, and on classic compute you pay the machines and the Databricks fee separately.

What just happened

    FAQ

    Questions people ask

    How do I make a Databricks to Power BI pipeline faster without streaming continuously?

    Chain the steps instead of waiting for clocks: the 15-minute run starts gold, and gold starts the Power BI refresh through the API. The screen then shows data about 6 to 22 minutes old instead of 30 to 90, for little extra cost, but Power BI needs PPU, Premium or Fabric capacity for that many refreshes.

    Is a continuous pipeline worth it for dashboards?

    Rarely. It brings the screen to about 2 to 4 minutes for roughly three to four times the cost. If someone must act in seconds, that belongs in the fast lane; if minutes are fine, chained 15-minute runs are enough.

    Is continuous Flink cheaper than continuous DLT?

    Usually, for small streams. Managed Flink can run on one or two small processing units (about $160 to $250 a month); the smallest continuous DLT pipeline is a driver and a worker (about $480 to $650 on classic compute, more on serverless). Per CPU-hour they are similar, so the gap shrinks as volume grows.

    Does a faster pipeline make an hour final sooner?

    No. An hour is final only when its slowest input arrives (lab results, nightly master data). A faster lane shows provisional numbers sooner; each hour keeps a 'complete' flag.
