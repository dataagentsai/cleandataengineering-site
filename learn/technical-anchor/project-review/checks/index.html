Project review · the catalog

The checks: all 210, by category

These are the questions the project review skills ask of a data project's documents, in three packs, one per discipline: 43 business analysis, 64 project management, 91 technical, plus 12 that only apply on Databricks. Each pack is also a skill on its own, and the full review checks the seams between them. Open a check to see why it matters, when it applies, what good evidence looks like, by which stage it is due, and the cheapest test that could prove a team wrong.

To review them: mark each check Keep, Change or Drop and add a note. Your marks stay in this browser. When you're done, Copy my review puts them on your clipboard as plain text to send back. The checks come from the repository, so this page always matches the skill.

Business analysis (43)

What the business needs, in terms that can be tested against data: requirements, definitions and metrics, data requirements, scope, acceptance.

Read first: Source semantic understanding: a key to data program success. What to ask about the meaning of each source (grain, statuses, corrections, dates, amounts, the number the business trusts) before anything is built.

Requirements, definitions, acceptance criteria (10)

REQ-01Vague words in the requirementshighBusiness analyst
Question
Has every vague word in the requirements, such as "real-time", "fast", "all data", "user-friendly", "accurate" or "complete", been replaced with a number or a test? If the users reject the system at the end, will it be because we each read those words differently?
Why it matters
"Real-time" meant within a second to the sponsor and by the next morning to the team. The design was built for daily loads and had to be rebuilt late in the project.
Applies to
any · components: any · patterns: any
Owner
Business analyst · also Technical anchor, Test lead
Evidence
in: requirements
looks like: Every quality word paired with a measure, for example "available by 7 a.m. on working days" instead of "timely", or "the last three years" instead of "all history".
How the gap shows
  • Words such as real-time, near real-time, fast, quickly, timely, all data, complete, accurate, user-friendly, intuitive, seamless, scalable or robust appear without a number next to them.
  • Lists of required data or reports end with "etc." or "and so on".
Compare
Compare the requirements with the architecture: if the requirement says "real-time" and the architecture schedules a daily batch, someone's reading of the word is wrong.
Due by
planstage 1: Requirement received
designedstage 4: Discovery sign-off
Fail fast
Ask the requester to put a number on each vague word in a fifteen-minute call, and write the answers down.

vague-words (found without AI)

REQ-02No acceptance criteriahighBusiness analyst
Question
Does every requirement have acceptance criteria that the data can be tested against? At user acceptance, how will we and the business agree that a requirement is met?
Why it matters
Requirements said "show monthly sales by region". At user acceptance the business compared it with their own report, got a different number, and nobody had agreed in advance which one was right.
Applies to
any · components: any · patterns: any
Owner
Business analyst · also Test lead, Technical anchor
Evidence
in: requirements, test-strategy
looks like: For each requirement, one or more criteria in the form "given this data, the result is this", ideally with a known figure the business already trusts.
How the gap shows
  • Requirements or user stories have no "acceptance criteria" or "done when" section.
  • Acceptance criteria repeat the requirement in other words rather than describing a test.
  • Backlog items are marked ready for build without criteria.
Compare
Compare the requirements with the test strategy: requirements that have no matching test, or tests that trace to no requirement, show the two were written apart.
Due by

Nobody asked how fresh the data had to be or how quickly it had to recover. After the first overnight failure the business found out it would take a day to recover, and the system was not accepted.

REQ-08Everything is a "must"highBusiness analyst
Question
Are the requirements prioritised (for example must, should, could and won't), with real items in the "won't" list and fewer musts than the first release can deliver? If time runs out, has the business already said which requirements go first?
Why it matters
Every one of sixty requirements was marked essential. When the build ran late the team cut by effort instead of value, dropped the one report the finance director actually used, and the release was rejected.
Applies to
any · components: any · patterns: any
Owner
Business analyst · also Business owner and sponsor, Project manager
Evidence
in: requirements, plan
looks like: Each requirement with a priority agreed by the business owner, a "won't this time" list with real items in it, and the musts sized to fit the first release with room to spare.
How the gap shows
  • No priority column in the requirements, or every requirement has the same priority.
  • The "won't" or "not this release" list is empty.
  • Musts make up most of the requirements.
Compare
Compare the must-have requirements with the first release in the plan: musts that are not in the first release, or a first release full of shoulds and coulds, mean priority and plan disagree.
Due by
planstage 3: Discovery
designedstage 4: Discovery sign-off

Ask the business owner: "If we could deliver only five of these, which five?" and record the answer as the priority.

A requirement for weekly churn by segment was dropped when the backlog was re-planned. Nothing linked it to the retention goal it served, so nobody noticed until the sponsor asked for the churn figure at closure.

SCOPE-01Scope written only as "in"highBusiness analyst
Question
Is there a written list of what is out of scope, as well as what is in? When someone says "I assumed that was included", which document will we point to?
Why it matters
The scope listed the tables to build. The business assumed the reports on top were included too, and the argument cost a month and the sponsor's trust.
Applies to
any · components: any · patterns: any
Owner
Business analyst · also Project manager, Technical anchor, Business owner and sponsor
Evidence
in: discovery, requirements
looks like: Three lists, in scope, out of scope and later, each item specific enough that a stranger could say which list a new request belongs to.
How the gap shows
  • No "out of scope" heading or list in the discovery, scope or requirements document.
  • The out-of-scope list is generic ("anything not listed above").

How the business gets the numbers today - reports replaced, trusted figures, manual adjustments, decisions served (4)

CUR-01The current process and reports not written downhighBusiness analyst
Question
Is the way the business produces this information today written down: which reports, spreadsheets, extracts and manual steps, who runs them, how often, and who reads them? If an old report keeps being used after go-live, will it be one we never knew existed?
Why it matters
The project replaced the monthly sales pack. Nobody had mapped the twelve spreadsheets that fed it, and one of them carried a manual rebate adjustment, so the new numbers were wrong by exactly that amount and the old pack stayed in use.
Applies to
any · components: modelling, serving · patterns: any
Owner
Business analyst · also Data consumers, Business owner and sponsor
Evidence
in: discovery, requirements
looks like: A current-state inventory: one row per report, spreadsheet or extract being replaced, with its producer, frequency, readers, the sources it pulls from and the manual steps in between.
How the gap shows
  • The discovery document describes the future solution but has no "current state" or "as is" section.
  • Reports to be replaced are mentioned by name only, with no producer, frequency or readers.
  • Phrases such as "replaces existing reporting" with no list of what that reporting is.
Compare
Compare the current-state inventory in the discovery document with the in-scope list: a report or spreadsheet in use today that is neither replaced nor declared out of scope will keep running beside the new system.
Due by
planstage 3: Discovery
designedstage 4: Discovery sign-off
Fail fast
Sit for one hour with the person who produces today's main report and write down every step, file and manual change they make.

Question
For each key figure, which report or person does the business trust today, and has the business agreed that the new system will be judged against it? When the new number differs from the old one, who will decide which is wrong?

The new revenue figure was two per cent lower than finance's ledger. Nobody had agreed in advance that the ledger was the reference, so three weeks were lost arguing about whose number was right before anyone looked for the cause.

  • The requirements say the new figures must be "accurate" or "correct" without saying compared with what.
  • The trusted figure is named but nobody has captured a value for a known period.
Compare

CUR-04Manual adjustments hidden in today's numbershighBusiness analyst
Question
Do today's trusted figures include manual adjustments, hand-kept mappings, overrides or exclusions that live in someone's spreadsheet or head, and has each one been listed with a decision to automate it, keep it manual, or drop it? If the new numbers never match the old ones, will it be because of a correction nobody told us about?
Why it matters
Each month an analyst removed two intercompany customers and reclassified a product line by hand before the board pack went out. The new system did neither, and it took a month of reconciliation to find out why.
Applies to
any · components: transformation, modelling · patterns: any
Owner
Business analyst · also Data consumers, Technical anchor
Evidence
in: discovery, requirements
looks like: A list of every manual adjustment, override, exclusion and hand-kept mapping in today's process, with its size, who makes it, why, and whether the new system will automate it, keep it as a governed manual input, or stop it.
How the gap shows
  • The current state is described as "extracted from the source system" with no manual steps.
  • Spreadsheets are mentioned as inputs to today's report with no description of what they change.
  • No requirement covers manual inputs, overrides or exclusions.
Compare
