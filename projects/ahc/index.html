AI Harness Catalog

An open list of the parts that must exist around a model call for production-grade AI.

1 · Purpose

The AI Harness Catalog (AHC) lists the components that must exist around a model call: context assembly, tool calls, retries, cost limits, human approval, logging and more, across sixteen layers. It is for architects and engineers building AI applications, and for teams choosing an agent framework. Teams often build logging and evaluation and call the job done, then meet outages in the layers they skipped. The catalog names every layer, says what breaks without each part, and shows what a chosen framework leaves for you to build.

For: AI architects, platform engineers, teams choosing or reviewing an agent framework.

2 · Business benefits

Fewer outages from missing partsEvery capability states the failure that follows when it is absent, so gaps are found at design time.
Know what a framework leaves outComparing a framework to the catalog shows what you still have to build before you commit.
Cheaper vendor changesPorts name the seams where the system meets outside services, so a provider can be swapped without a rewrite.
Linked to testingEach capability names the assurance obligations it helps meet, so build and test plans line up.

4 · Requirements

What it must do

  • Lists 118 capabilities with permanent ids across 16 harness layers and 10 kinds of application.
  • States for each capability what it must do, what breaks without it, and the design decisions it forces.
  • Defines 18 ports, the seams where a harness meets a service it does not own.
  • Provides a profile format in which a team declares what it built, what it accepts as a gap and what does not apply.
  • Links each capability to the AI Assurance Catalog obligations it helps meet.
  • Ships blueprints for two kinds of application: document extraction pipelines and tool-using agents.

How well it must do it

  • It describes components and never ships them as a library.
  • A linter rejects verification words in a requirement, so build and test statements stay in separate catalogs.
  • It depends on the assurance catalog in one direction only.
  • Named products come last, because they change faster than the requirements.
  • Specification text is CC BY 4.0 and tooling is Apache 2.0; releases carry a DOI.
