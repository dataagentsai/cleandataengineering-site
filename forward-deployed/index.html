The Forward Deployed Engineer roadmap for data and AI

A forward deployed engineer sits with a customer, finds the real problem and ships a working, evaluated, costed system in the customer's own environment, then hands it over. For data and AI that means being part data engineer, part AI engineer, part software engineer, and part technical anchor, business analyst and delivery lead. This page is the map: our recommended roadmap, and a way into every guide on the site.

Jump to a track: Data engineeringPlatform, cost, securityAI engineeringSoftware engineeringTechnical anchorBusiness analysisAgile and deliveryCustomer craft

The role on one picture

Four kinds of skill, one outcome

The job is the combination, not any one part. A data platform with no discovery solves the wrong problem; a clever agent on poor data fails in use; a good build the customer cannot run is not finished.

Eight tracks in four clusters. Each track has a card below with what to be able to do and where to learn it.

Our recommendation

The roadmap we would follow, and why

Do not learn the tracks one after another. Learn them in parallel at rising depth, against a real engagement, with one piece of evidence at the end of each phase. Seven principles sit behind that.

PrincipleWhyWhat to do
Deep in data, working across the restA T shape. Data engineering is the deep stroke because AI systems are only as good as the data under them. The other tracks need working skill, not mastery.Pick data as your depth; reach 'build' level in every other track before aiming for 'own'.
Parallel tracks, not a queueA forward deployed engineer is never only technical. If customer craft waits until year two, you build the wrong thing very well.Touch discovery, estimation and writing from week one, in small doses.
Learn against one real engagementCourses teach vocabulary; a capstone teaches decisions. Choose one realistic problem and let it pull the learning.Keep one capstone repository and one decision log for the whole roadmap.
Deterministic firstAI engineering sits on top of data and software engineering; it does not replace them. Use code where code works and a model only where judgement on language is needed.Before adding a model, write what plain code could do and what it cannot.
Evidence over demosDecision makers approve what they can check. Every phase should end with an artifact someone else can read.End each phase with a one-page evidence note: what you built, how you measured it, what it costs.
One platform deep, others by ideaTools change; ideas last. Go deep on one cloud and one data platform and learn the others as concepts.Choose your platform from the learning paths, and map every concept to it once.
Teach it backWriting an idea in plain words shows exactly where you do not understand it, and is how you earn trust with customers.Write one short note per week. Publish the good ones.

When to learn what

Eight tracks across four phases

The grid shows how deep to go in each phase: taste (know it exists and try it), build (do it with help), own (do it alone and teach it). Click a track name to jump to its card.

Depth by phase. Foundation leans on data and software; Build adds AI; Deliver and Lead are customer-facing. Time spans are rules of thumb.

1. Foundation

about months 0 to 3

Be a safe pair of hands on data and code. Data engineering and software engineering come first, because everything later stands on them. Touch every other track lightly.

You are ready when
  • You have built a small pipeline from raw to gold, with tests, in git.
  • You can explain why a table is laid out the way it is.
  • You can write a customer problem in one paragraph.

Leave behind: One small pipeline repository and a one-page problem statement.

2. Build

about months 3 to 6

Add AI engineering and shape the platform. Build an agent with tools and a human approval step, and evaluate it on agreed cases. Learn what it costs per run. Start discovery and estimation practice.

You are ready when
  • You have an agent with an evaluation set and a cost per case.
  • You can say what the model will not do, and why.
  • You can read a slow or expensive job and name the bucket.

Leave behind: An agent, its evaluation report and its cost page.

3. Deliver

about months 6 to 12

Run a real engagement, from discovery to hand-over. Discovery, prototype in days, deploy into the customer's cloud, show evidence, write the runbook. This is where the customer-facing tracks become the main work.

You are ready when
  • It runs in their account, not yours.
  • You can show quality and cost on one page.
  • Their engineers run it without you.

Leave behind: An engagement case study with the evidence page and runbook.

4. Lead

year 2 onward

Make the second customer faster than the first. Reusable assets with a port and an adapter per customer, architecture ownership, mentoring, and saying no with evidence.

You are ready when
  • A second customer reuses your framework with a new configuration.
  • Others rely on your reviews and templates.
  • You choose which work not to do.

Leave behind: A reusable framework and a design review you led.

Months are a guide for someone working on this steadily. If you already work in data engineering, Foundation compresses a lot. The checkpoints matter more than the calendar.

Writing, explaining, showing evidence, handing over. The skill that turns a good build into an engagement the customer keeps.

What to leave for later

Temptations, with a verdict

TemptationVerdictWhy
Learning every toolDropTools churn. Learn one platform deep and the rest as concepts.
Collecting certifications before buildingDelayBuild something first; take a certification when it confirms what you already do.
Fine-tuning models earlyDelayMost first problems are solved by data, prompts, tools and evaluation. Fine-tune only when an evaluation shows the need.
Hopping between agent frameworksDropLearn the loop, tools, state and approval ideas once; frameworks are wrappers.

Slide-first demosDropShow a working thing on their data, then the evidence page.
Streaming for everythingDelayReal time costs more. Use it only where the business acts within seconds.

Certifications

Where they fit

Build first, certify to confirm. A sensible order: a platform or cloud certification near the end of Build, an AI certification once you have an agent with an evaluation, and an architect-level certification after your first Deliver. Options by platform are on the certifications page, and the forward deployed learning path lists them per cloud.

FAQ

Questions people ask

What is a forward deployed engineer, in this roadmap?

An engineer who sits with a customer, finds the real problem and ships a working, evaluated, costed system in the customer's own environment, then hands it over. Here it covers data, AI, software, technical anchor, business analysis and agile delivery.

Do I need to be expert in all eight tracks?

No. Be deep in one, usually data engineering, and reach working level in the others. Own the customer-facing tracks by the Deliver phase because that is where engagements are won or lost.

How long does this take?

The spans are rules of thumb for someone working on it steadily. If you already work in data, the Foundation phase compresses a lot. If you are starting out, expect it to stretch. The checkpoints matter more than the months.

Data first or AI first?

Data first, in small doses of AI from the start. AI built on weak data and weak software is the usual cause of agents that demo well and fail in use.

Where do certifications fit?

After you have built something. Use them to confirm a level, not to reach it. The certifications page lists the options by platform.

Is this the only valid roadmap?

No. It is our opinion, shaped by the pages on this site. Use the tracks as a menu and change the order to fit the work in front of you.
