Billing

How Databricks charges you: the formula and the two invoices

Everything on Databricks compute is priced by one formula:

Databricks charge = DBUs used × price per DBU.

A DBU (Databricks Unit) is a unit of processing per hour. A machine or warehouse "burns" a fixed number of DBUs per hour depending on its type and size. The price per DBU depends on the product (SKU), your plan tier (Premium or Enterprise), the cloud, the region and your contract.

What changes between classic and serverless is what else you pay for separately.

What else is billed

  • Per-second billing. Classic VMs and DBUs are billed per second while the cluster is up. Starting, running, idle and waiting-to-terminate all count.
  • Pool VMs. Idle VMs in a pool cost you cloud money but no DBUs.
  • Photon. On classic clusters Photon raises the DBU rate for those machines. Serverless and SQL warehouses already include it.
  • Other products with their own units. Model serving (per hour or per token), Vector Search, Lakebase databases, pipelines, predictive optimization, and so on. Each is its own SKU family (see SKU families).
  • Commit discounts. Large customers buy a committed spend with a negotiated discount. The invoice reflects it. Some system tables only show list prices (see what is hidden).

How many SKUs?

There is no honest single number. A SKU is a billing line, and Databricks creates separate SKUs for each product, plan tier (Premium, Enterprise), cloud, and for many serverless products, each region. On one cloud it is in the order of a hundred or more, and across all clouds, regions and tiers the price list holds several hundred rows. You can count your own exactly:

SELECT COUNT(DISTINCT sku_name) FROM system.billing.list_prices;

And to see only what you actually used: SELECT DISTINCT sku_name FROM system.billing.usage;

Learn them as families, which is how they are named:

Autoscaling and what it does to cost

Autoscaling means the cluster grows and shrinks between a minimum and a maximum. Cost follows the number of nodes at each moment. A cluster with min 2 and max 10 workers that sits at 8 workers most of the day costs four times a fixed 2-worker cluster, even though your "cluster size" setting looks small.

ComputeHow it scalesIs the scaling visible to you?
Classic clusterWorkers added or removed within min and maxYes, with work. The cluster event log shows RESIZING events with the target worker count. system.compute.node_timeline gives per-minute utilisation per node. system.compute.clusters shows config changes. But system.billing.usage is hourly per cluster, so you must stitch events and billing together to see which minutes cost what.
Classic / Pro / Serverless SQL warehouseAdds or removes whole clusters between min and maxYes. system.compute.warehouse_events records starting, running, scaled up, scaled down and stopping with the cluster count.
Serverless jobs, notebooks, pipelinesDatabricks decides, you cannot see itNo. You see DBUs consumed and run duration. You do not see node counts, node types or scale decisions. This is a real visibility gap.

So your point is right for serverless, and half right for classic. Classic gives you the raw events but not a ready-made "cost of this scale-up" number. Serverless gives you the DBU total but no scaling history at all.

Practical consequences: on classic, check "peak workers" and "average workers" per job and compare with min and max. On serverless, compare DBU per run over time and treat a rising trend as the only signal of over-scaling.

Why people insist on classic "for billing tracing"

The claim has a true core and an outdated shell. The true core: on classic compute, cost can be traced in two places that agree with each other, and at machine level.

Reason people giveStill true today?
Cloud bill (AWS Cost Explorer, Azure Cost Management) shows VMs tagged per cluster, so finance can reconcile against Databricks.True. Serverless has no VMs in your account to tag.
Per-cluster granularity: one cluster per team makes cost obvious.True, and a cluster is a natural unit. Serverless jobs and notebooks have no such object.
You can see machine utilisation and prove whether you are wasting money.True on classic. Not available on serverless.
Serverless notebooks could not be tagged per team.Largely outdated. Serverless budget policies now let an admin attach tags to all serverless usage of a user, group or service principal, and those tags appear in system.billing.usage. Warehouses and jobs can also carry tags.
Show a Databricks cost and a cloud cost side by side to justify a commit.True. With serverless you only have the Databricks number, so you cannot see the split between Databricks margin and the machine cost inside it.

My reading: classic is the better choice when finance demands a machine-level, two-bill audit trail. For most teams, serverless with budget policies and tagged warehouses is enough, and the tracing gap is smaller than the idle and boot waste classic carries.
