Find where the money goes before you tune anything

Most of a Databricks bill comes from a small number of jobs, warehouses and clusters. Tuning the long tail first wastes effort. Rank your spend, fix the top few, then measure again.

SELECT usage_metadata.job_id, usage_metadata.warehouse_id, usage_metadata.cluster_id, SUM(usage_quantity) AS dbus FROM system.billing.usage WHERE usage_date >= current_date() - 30 GROUP BY ALL ORDER BY dbus DESC LIMIT 20

Run this once a month from a small incremental copy rather than against the system tables directly, as explained on the system tables page. Multiply DBUs by the list price for a rough dollar figure, and remember that list price is not your contract price.

A monthly routine

  1. List the top 10 jobs, warehouses and clusters by DBUs.
  2. For each, check idle time and CPU or memory use (system.compute.node_timeline) and failed or retried runs.
  3. Look for zombies: jobs nobody reads, warehouses that never stop, clusters left running.
  4. Fix the top three. Measure again next month.

Pipelines

Pipelines and streaming

  • Run non-real-time streaming as scheduled batch with the available-now trigger. An always-on stream keeps a cluster up around the clock.
  • Prefer triggered mode over continuous for pipelines. Databricks says continuous mode needs an always-on cluster and is significantly more expensive. If you do need continuous, Databricks recommends running it as a continuous job.
  • Use production mode for scheduled runs. In development mode a pipeline reuses a long-running cluster across updates and does not retry on errors, so idle time can build up. A forum answer from a Databricks employee gives a 2-hour default shutdown delay for development mode, but I could not confirm that in the official docs, and the official properties page says auto-termination cannot be overridden. Do not depend on a shutdown setting without testing it.
  • Read only new data with Auto Loader, the change data feed or incremental materialized views, instead of reloading everything.

Data

Data and layout: cheaper because they are faster

  • Filter early and select only the columns you need. Scanning less data is the cheapest speed-up.
  • Fix the layout. Liquid clustering or sensible partitioning, regular compaction, no tiny files. See why writes are slow.
  • Avoid rewriting whole tables. Append where you can, and use MERGE with deletion vectors for changes.
  • Remove the usual accidents: Python UDFs, collect(), skewed joins and accidental cross joins.
  • Z-order on huge tables is expensive to maintain. Liquid clustering is usually cheaper.
  • Compare Predictive Optimization with your own scheduled OPTIMIZE, and only maintain tables that are actually queried.

Governance

Tags, budgets and commitments

  • Tag everything and enforce it with cluster policies and budget policies. You cannot cut what you cannot attribute. See tagging best practices.
  • Size commitments to your steady baseline, not your peak. A commit discount only helps if you use it, and an over-commit wastes money.
  • Use savings plans or reserved instances for always-on VMs on classic compute.
  • Check whether Databricks spend counts toward your existing cloud commitment if you buy through a cloud marketplace.
  • Join the Databricks bill to the cloud bill. The system tables show DBUs at list price and exclude your VMs and storage. See how billing works.
